Part VI · Proofs of knowledge

How can two answers reveal the secret witness?

The previous simulator rewound a verifier to create a transcript without the witness. Now consider the reverse question: could we rewind a prover that successfully answers challenges and extract a witness? This would establish a stronger property than ordinary soundness. Soundness says a cheater shouldn't convince us of a false claim. But sometimes the statement is already known to be true, and we want more: evidence that this particular prover actually knows the secret witness.

Knowledge needs a different experiment

A knowledge extractor \(\mathrm{Ext}\) is an efficient algorithm allowed to interact with a possibly dishonest prover \(P^\ast\). It can save the prover's state after its first message, then rewind to that exact state and ask a different challenge. An ordinary verifier does not have this power.

The course gives a probability guarantee connecting the prover's chance of being accepted to the extractor's chance of returning a valid witness \(w\), where \(M(x,w)=1\).

\[\Pr\!\left[M(x,w)=1:\ w\leftarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{P^\ast}(x)\right]\ \geq\ \Pr[\langle P^\ast,V\rangle(x)=1]-\varepsilon\]

On the left is the probability of successfully extracting a witness. On the right is the prover's chance of acceptance minus the allowed knowledge error \(\varepsilon\). The superscript \(P^\ast\) means the extractor is running that same possibly dishonest prover.

Construct the extractor for the Hamiltonicity protocol

For the graph protocol, suppose the prover could answer either challenge correctly. The extractor saves the state immediately after the prover committed to graph \(B\) and permutation \(\Pi\). It asks \(c=0\), learning the full graph and \(\Pi\). Then it rewinds and asks \(c=1\), learning a Hamiltonian cycle of the very same committed \(B\).

Which information becomes extractable after rewinding?

One response alone doesn't reveal the secret: the full graph opening gives the permutation but not necessarily a cycle; the cycle opening gives a cycle of the renamed graph but hides the renaming. The extractor obtains both.

Why extraction works

From \(c=0\), we know \(B_{ij}=1\iff\{\Pi(i),\Pi(j)\}\in E\). From \(c=1\), we have a cycle \((b_1,\ldots,b_n,b_1)\) in \(B\). Apply the permutation \(\Pi\) to each \(b_i\). Different \(b_i\) remain different because \(\Pi\) is one-to-one, and every edge maps back to a real edge of \(G\). So the result is a Hamiltonian cycle of \(G\).

Do not confuse the two kinds of rewinding

Notice the reversal. A zero-knowledge simulator rewinds the verifier and creates a transcript without the witness. A knowledge extractor rewinds the prover and recovers a real witness.

This construction assumes the prover successfully answers the challenges used for extraction. For a prover that succeeds only sometimes, a full extraction theorem also has to account for that success probability.

Homework 3 · Problem 1(a)

Can a proof of knowledge have knowledge error below the soundness error?

Suppose some cheating prover convinces the honest verifier of a false statement with probability \(p>1/3\). Could this still be a proof of knowledge with knowledge error \(\kappa<1/3\)?

Derive the contradiction from the definition

If \(x\notin L\) is false, there is no valid witness, so extraction must succeed with probability zero. The knowledge guarantee then gives \(0\ge p-\kappa\), meaning \(p\le\kappa\). Thus a false-statement success probability \(p>1/3\) is incompatible with knowledge error \(\kappa<1/3\).

The same pattern—two accepted responses to different challenges, with one fixed first message—can be stated as a general property. That's what Sigma protocols do.
