Part VI · Proofs of knowledge

Rewinding the prover can reveal the witness

A soundness guarantee says a dishonest prover cannot usually convince an honest verifier that a false claim is true. But consider a statement that is trivially true for everyone: soundness alone says nothing about whether the prover personally knows the secret that an authentication protocol is meant to test.

Knowledge needs a different experiment

We define a knowledge extractor, \(\mathrm{Ext}\), as an efficient algorithm that can interact with a potentially dishonest prover \(P^\ast\) and rewind that prover. Unlike the ordinary verifier, an extractor may restore the prover to a saved internal state and ask a different challenge for the same initial commitment.

For a statement \(x\), a witness \(w\), and an efficient witness checker \(M\), the lecture's proof-of-knowledge guarantee has the form:

\[\Pr\!\left[M(x,w)=1:\ w\leftarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{P^\ast}(x)\right]\ \geq\ \Pr[\langle P^\ast,V\rangle(x)=1]-\varepsilon\]

The left side is the probability that extraction returns a valid witness; the right side is the malicious prover's acceptance probability minus the allowed knowledge error \(\varepsilon\). The superscript \(P^\ast\) indicates that the extractor interacts with that prover.

Construct the extractor for the Hamiltonicity protocol

Assume first that the prover always succeeds at both challenges. The extractor saves the prover's state immediately after it sends commitments to \(B\) and \(\pi\). It requests \(c=0\), obtains the full permutation \(\pi\) and graph \(B\), and checks them. Then it rewinds to the same saved state and requests \(c=1\), obtaining a Hamiltonian cycle of that same committed graph \(B\).

Why extraction works

Challenge 0 proves \(B=\pi(G)\). Challenge 1 supplies a Hamiltonian cycle \((b_1,\ldots,b_n,b_1)\) of \(B\). Apply \(\pi^{-1}\) to each vertex to obtain \((\pi^{-1}(b_1),\ldots,\pi^{-1}(b_n),\pi^{-1}(b_1))\). Because \(\pi\) is one-to-one, these are distinct original vertices; because \(\pi\) preserves edges, every consecutive pair is an edge of \(G\). So the extractor outputs a Hamiltonian cycle of \(G\), which is a valid witness.

Do not confuse the two kinds of rewinding

A ZK simulator rewinds the verifier to make a witness-free fake view. A knowledge extractor rewinds the prover to obtain two answers and recover an actual witness. Their powers serve opposing security goals.

For a prover that does not always succeed, a full knowledge-extraction analysis must also account for the probability of getting two accepting replies. We have proved the core extraction mechanism under the always-successful assumption, just as the lecture's worked argument does.

This “two accepting answers to one commitment” pattern is so useful that cryptographers isolate it as an abstract property. That gives the next concept: a Sigma protocol.
