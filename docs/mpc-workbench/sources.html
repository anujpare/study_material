Sources and teaching scope

The learning path follows the printed lecture’s order, with homework inserted after its prerequisites. Each card contains a bounded question. New practice questions are authored for this lab; they are not presented as past MPC exam questions.

Supplied course materials

  • lecture17-18-19_mpc.pdf: 11 pages, Privacy Enhancing Technologies FS2026, Florian Tramèr. Defines MPC and develops additive sharing, GMW-style arithmetic evaluation, Beaver triples, malicious security and Shamir sharing.
  • lecture17-18-19_notes.pdf: 17 handwritten pages. Reviewed alongside the printed notes, including the marked difficulty with Beaver’s algebra and the interpolation derivation.
  • hw6_sol.pdf: 6 pages, Exercise Sheet 6, version 1, FS2025. All 16 subquestions appear with their assignment labels and worked reasoning.
  • Privacy Enhancing Technologies HS 2025 Endterm (Solution).pdf: 8 supplied pages. This paper concerns differential privacy. It calibrates the scope of one-sentence counterexamples, short derivations and staged proofs; it contains no MPC exam questions.

What the experiments establish

Small finite-field examples expose the mechanics and errors. They are not cryptographic software, empirical security estimates or substitutes for the general proofs. A full malicious MPC implementation, pairing implementation, and security proof of distributed preprocessing are outside the supplied lectures.

Written solutions use self-check criteria. Numerical and choice questions are checked automatically. Progress and draft answers are stored only in this browser’s local storage; the site sends no answers to a server.

Clarifications to terse source arguments

  • Lecture p. 4: expand additive privacy to the joint distribution of every coalition of n−1 shares; uniformity of one sum or each separate share is not by itself a joint-view proof.
  • Lecture pp. 2–3 and 8: privacy permits all inferences from corrupt inputs and the agreed output. It does not mean that no honest input can ever be inferred after output reconstruction.
  • Lecture p. 6: the trusted dealer is an additional noncollusion and observation assumption. A dealer retaining a and obtaining delta=x−a recovers x; merely having no function input does not itself imply trust.
  • Lecture pp. 6–7: Beaver triples need independently uniform fresh masks, independently randomized additive sharings, and one-time use. The public delta·epsilon correction is added to exactly one component.
  • Lecture p. 8: the full simulator must preserve the relationships among corrupt inputs, random coins, masked broadcasts, intermediate components and the supplied final output. Independently sampling every message as a uniform value is not a valid full simulation.
  • Lecture pp. 7–8: information-theoretic online security assumes ideal correct fresh triples and appropriate private channels. Encryption-based preprocessing makes the complete construction computationally secure; threshold key generation and decryption-to-shares are an outline in these notes.
  • Lecture p. 8: communication proportional to multiplication count describes gate evaluation for fixed party count. Input distribution, output reconstruction, preprocessing and the cost of implementing a broadcast are additional; communication volume is distinct from multiplication-layer latency.
  • All numerical core examples use F17. These examples teach field arithmetic and are not secure parameter choices or an implementation of an actual cryptographic protocol.
  • HS2025 Endterm contains differential privacy only. It informs short subquestion scope, counterexample and derivation practice, but supplies no actual MPC exam questions.
  • HW6 1(b): the supplied FALSE answer assumes a reliable additional disclosure/equality certification. Merely outputting one's own input, which happens to equal the other when equal, would not establish disclosure; equality may also be an allowed functionality output.
  • HW6 2(b): commit-then-open with hiding/binding supplies the intended fixed-before-cleartext-opening argument and may abort. Generic hiding/binding alone does not establish non-malleability or fully independent committed inputs; copied commitments/openings illustrate the gap. Full ideal-channel realization needs suitable commitment functionality. This is separate from MPC output fairness.
  • HW6 2(c): a fixed nonzero offset forces a wrong field result. For a wrong output bit specifically, use the Boolean circuit over F2 and offset one.
  • HW6 2(d): computing a product tag via Beaver multiplication establishes its algebraic invariant; it is not a complete malicious-security proof. Preprocessing and intermediate openings must also be protected.
  • HW6 2(e): individual opened check shares need not be zero; only their sum is zero. Key privacy requires correct randomized authenticated-share distributions, with an explicit zero-sum transcript argument.
  • HW6 2(e): check messages must be fixed before others are revealed; otherwise a rushing sender cancels their sum without knowing the key. One logical field element over the fair channel is not one physical message when commit/open implements the channel.
  • HW6 2(e): for a nonzero offset and uniform hidden key, the needed correction is Delta times the key; an attacker may guess successfully with probability 1/p, so success does not literally imply key knowledge.
  • HW6 3(b): the two displayed square identities are equivalent. The online field-element payload halves from two openings to one, while both versions can use one opening round.
  • HW6 3(c): powers a^0 and x^0 are public one and must be shared consistently, e.g. (1,0,...,0). The constant-polynomial case d=0 needs no preprocessing or opening. Never reuse the same mask for another private input.
  • Shamir generation at positions 1,...,n needs p>n (or any n distinct nonzero field positions). The lecture omits this explicit size condition.
  • The interpolation and Shamir degree bounds are at most d-1 and at most t-1. Sampled leading coefficients may equal zero; excluding zero changes the privacy distribution.
  • Handwritten p. 15 draws a smooth real curve as intuition. The lab uses discrete finite-field evaluations and labels the field.
  • Handwritten p. 17 writes the inverse/coefficient relationship informally. The lesson uses column vectors and the correct orientation a=V^{-1}y.
  • HW6 1(c) has two independent problems: repeated first coordinates may be incompatible, and distinct coordinates guarantee degree at most d rather than exactly d.
  • The Shamir privacy proof concerns the full joint view of t-1 shares. It counts one coefficient list for each fixed secret and view, yielding probability p^{-(t-1)}; insufficient reconstruction alone is not a privacy proof.
  • Threshold t counts required shares here. Ordinary Shamir sharing neither authenticates malformed shares nor guarantees availability or fairness.
  • The handwritten final-page linearity observation supports addition and public scaling. Local products have degree at most 2(t-1), so their direct reconstruction generally needs 2t-1 points; the supplied notes do not develop a degree-reduction MPC protocol.
  • HW6 4(c) uses trusted-dealer key generation, not distributed key generation. Perfect secrecy of scalar Shamir shares does not imply perfect secrecy of published group encodings or by itself prove threshold-signature unforgeability; security remains conditional on the intended BLS assumptions.
  • HW6 4(d) checks each partial signature against its own established public key share and the same message. Rejecting a faulty share does not guarantee that t valid shares arrive.
